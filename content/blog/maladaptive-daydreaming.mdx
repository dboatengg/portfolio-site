Daydreaming has always been my biggest obstacle: I constantly create scenarios in my head, with myself as the hero or main character, seeking for validation.

## The Loop I Couldn't Escape

I've caught myself lost in obsessive, repetitive daydreams several times, but one recent incident really got me to stop and question what was happening.

Several weeks ago, there were people who worked near where I stay who used to gossip and spread false rumors about me.

I used to spend a lot of time imagining them realizing the truth: that I'm not who they think I am, that they're wrong about me, and that I'm actually a "good guy."

It was a strange, exhausting loop that was very hard for me to escape, and it left me more drained and isolated each time it happened.

So I started to wonder why my mind kept repeating these loops, and decided to look into it more.

## Why My Brain Kept Doing This

It turned out that what I've been experiencing all these years has a name: **maladaptive daydreaming**, sometimes called **excessive daydreaming**. It's when daydreaming becomes so frequent or intense that it starts interfering with your actual life (Wikipedia).

Experts don't know exactly why it happens, but they suspect it's a coping mechanism tied to things like anxiety, ADHD, OCD, or depression.

The simple explanation is this: you were hurt, disrespected, or misjudged, and your mind keeps replaying the scene, this time with you winning. It's just your brain trying to make you feel better or in control of the situation.

These daydreams feel emotionally real and rewarding, so your brain starts craving them. Daydreaming can feel like you're working through the pain you experienced, but really, it just keeps you emotionally invested in what others think of you.

Over time, you start to feel more comfortable in the daydream than in real life. Escaping into a made-up story feels easier than dealing with tough feelings. The more you do it, the more natural it feels to hide there, and the harder it becomes to stay present and enjoy real life.

## What Actually Helped

Once I understood what was happening, I started searching for practical ways to break out of this daydreaming loop. 

Here's what's worked for me:

**1. Name it the moment it starts**

When I catch myself daydreaming, I say to myself: "I'm about to make a movie in my head again." That simple sentence snaps me out of autopilot.

By naming what my brain is doing, I move from reacting emotionally to actually noticing what's happening. I stop playing a role in the daydream and just watch it, without getting pulled in.

That small moment of awareness is powerful. It gives me a chance to choose how to respond instead of getting carried away by whatever story my mind is trying to tell.

**2. Be vulnerable about what actually happened**

Instead of rewriting the story in my head into a version where I win, I just try to sit with what actually happened.

If I'm constantly replaying the situation in my head, defending myself and analyzing it from different angles, that's not processing, that's just rehearsing. And it keeps the charge alive instead of burning it down.

Processing looks more like sitting with "this really hurt me," without adding anything to it. No story in my head, no imagined moment where they finally see they were wrong. Just staring at the thing itself.

If this is done enough, it gets to a point where my mind either stops bringing the situation up at all, or even if it does, I'm unaffected by it. Not because I changed what happened in the past, but because I've already been there and already processed or healed from it.

## Choosing Real Life
Maladaptive daydreaming very often convinces me I’m healing and moving on from my past negative experiences, but really, it’s just my brain’s way of avoiding real growth.

As I get better at noticing the daydream starting and labeling the thoughts behind it, I'm learning to bring my focus back to the present moment. I'm putting my mental energy into real projects and real experiences now, instead of getting lost in daydreams.
